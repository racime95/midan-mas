Annual Planning
📅 What is periodization? ›

Periodization is the systematic organization of training across a season to ensure athletes peak at the right moment — for the most important competition. Without periodization, athletes either arrive at competitions under-prepared or burnt out.

Core principle: Training cannot always be at maximum intensity. The body needs cycles of loading and recovery to adapt and improve.
The 8 training periods used in this system:
CodePeriodPurposeTypical Intensity
PBBase PeriodBuild aerobic base, fundamental technique40–60%
PP1Preparation 1Introduce sport-specific load55–70%
PP2Preparation 2Increase specificity and volume65–80%
PP3Preparation 3High intensity, competition simulation75–90%
PTTransfer PeriodBridge between phases, active recovery40–55%
PPFPerformance PeriodNear-peak, final competition preparation80–95%
COMPCompetitionActive competition phase50–70%
PMMaintenanceMaintain fitness, reduce fatigue50–65%
Common error: Do not code a taper week as PPF. A taper reduces stimulus — use PT or REST instead. PPF implies near-peak training load.
🗓️ The 52-week season structure ›

The MAS Pathway season runs from Week 1 (30 Aug 2026) to Week 52 (26 Aug 2027). Each week runs Sunday → Thursday, with Friday–Saturday as the Qatari weekend.

WeeksMonthTypical Phase
W1–W4September 2026PB — Season opening, baseline tests
W5–W8October 2026PP1 — First preparation block
W9–W16Nov–Dec 2026PP2/PP3 — Build phase
W17–W20January 2027PPF/COMP — First competition block
W21–W24February 2027PM/PT — Recovery and rebuild
W25–W32Mar–Apr 2027PP2→PPF — Second preparation block
W33–W36May 2027COMP — Peak competition period
W37–W44Jun–Jul 2027PM — Maintenance and transition
W45–W48July–Aug 2027REST/PB — Season close
W49–W52August 2027Extended — Competitions & training camps
Week 5 (5–11 October) is the baseline test week for all age groups. This is the reference point for all Q1 KPI monitoring in December.
⚡ Physical capacities: H / M / L ›

Each week you indicate the emphasis for 7 physical capacities: H (High emphasis), M (Medium), L (Low), or — (not trained this week).

CapacityCodeBest developed inU10/U12 guidance
SpeedSPPP2, PP3, PPFM — keep playful
PowerPWPP2, PPFL — avoid heavy loads
StrengthSTPP1, PP2L — bodyweight only
EnduranceENPB, PP1M — games-based
FlexibilityFLAll periodsH — critical at this stage
CoordinationCOAll periodsH — #1 priority
AgilityAGPP1 to PPFH — multi-directional
Taper check: During a taper week (PT or REST), no more than 1 capacity at H. Multiple H ratings during a taper contradicts the purpose of reducing stimulus.
🏋️ Training methods: how many sessions per week? ›

For each of the 9 training methods, enter the number of sessions you plan per week (0 = not included).

Rule: The sum of all method sessions should match your total planned sessions for the week.
Example
Athletics U14
Week 7 (PP1): 4 sessions total
Sprint/Speed Work: 2 · Technical Drills: 1 · Strength & Jumps: 1 · Coordination & Agility: 1 · Flexibility & Mobility: 1
→ Total: 6 method slots across 4 sessions (some sessions combine methods)
Multi-event Sampling (method 7) is essential for U10 and U12 throughout the year — it ensures LTAD-compliant sport sampling.

SMART Objectives
🎯 What is a SMART objective? ›

A SMART objective is a formal commitment that combines five elements. Without all five, the objective cannot be monitored or evaluated.

LetterMeaningQuestion it answers
SSpecificWho exactly? Which group? Which skill?
MMeasurableWhat number or score proves success?
AAchievableIs this realistic given the baseline?
RRelevantDoes it match this group's LTAD stage?
TTime-boundBy exactly when? Which competition or date?
Not an objective: "Improve the team's performance." → No measurement, no deadline, no specific group.
Not an objective: "Increase attendance." → No target value, no timeline.
Template: "By [precise date or competition], all [age group] athletes will achieve [indicator] of [target value] through [main training approach]."
✍️ Sport-specific objective examples ›
Basketball — U12 — Performance objective
Basketball U12
"By end of Q2 (March 2027, Week 26), all U12 players achieve a dribbling slalom time of 8.5 s or below and a shooting accuracy of 40% or above from close range, through systematic skill repetition and progressive small-sided game exposure."

KPIs linked: BSK-U12-1 (dribbling), BSK-U12-2 (shooting %)
Fencing — U10 — Behavioral objective
Fencing U10
"By end of Q2 (March 2027), all U10 fencers maintain an enjoyment and engagement score of 4/5 or above at every monthly assessment and participate in a minimum of 2 multi-sport activities per month, through a 100% games-based approach with no performance pressure."

KPIs linked: FEN-U10-3 (enjoyment), FEN-U10-6 (multi-sport)
Volleyball — U14 — Performance objective
Volleyball U14
"By the first Priority A competition of the season (W17, January 2027), all U14 players achieve a free ball passing score of 8/10 or above and a spike ball control score of 5/8 or above, through systematic integration of the full 5/1 game system."

KPIs linked: VBL-U14-2 (free ball pass), VBL-U14-3 (spike control)
Tennis — U16 — Individual athlete objective
Tennis U16
"By end of season (August 2027), Dana Al Raei achieves a match win rate of 50% or above in official competition and a first serve percentage of 60% or above in match conditions, through an individual performance plan built around her ITF ranking progression."

KPIs linked: TEN-U16-7 (match win rate), TEN-U16-3 (first serve %)
Write one objective per active age group. If you have both U14 and U16 athletes training together, write two separate objectives — the expectations for each group are different.
🔗 Linking objectives to KPIs ›
1
Write the objective first
Start with the outcome you want by the end of the season. Be precise about the group, the measure, the target value and the deadline.
2
Identify which KPI measures this outcome
Look at your KPI Framework. The objective mentions "first serve percentage 60%+" — which KPI row tracks first serve %? That is your linked KPI.
3
Enter the KPI code in the objective form
In the "Linked KPI Codes" field, enter the code (e.g. TEN-U16-3). You can link to multiple KPIs if the objective is composite.
4
Fill the KPI baseline after Week 5 tests
Go to the KPI Framework and enter the individual athlete starting values in the Coach Notes column. Without this, you cannot prove progression at the Q1 review.
A KPI without a parent objective tells you how much but not toward what. It is data, not a management tool.

KPI Framework
📊 What is a KPI and why does it matter? ›

A KPI (Key Performance Indicator) is a quantified measure that tracks progress toward an objective. It answers: "Are we moving in the right direction, and how fast?"

A good KPI has four elements: a clear indicator name · a measurable benchmark · a measurement method · a defined frequency
TypeWhat it measuresAppropriate for
PerformanceResults: times, scores, rankingsU14, U16
ProcessTechnique quality, executionU12, U14
BehavioralAttendance, enjoyment, engagementU10, U12
HealthInjury-free weeks, recoveryAll groups
U10 athletes should have 0 performance KPIs. Ranking or scoring children at the FUNdamentals stage is developmentally inappropriate per LTAD (Balyi et al., 2013).
📏 How to set a benchmark ›
1
Run the Week 5 baseline test
The first test measures where each athlete starts. This is your reference point — never skip it. Record individual results, not just the group average.
2
Set a realistic progression target
Based on the baseline, set a target that is achievable by Q1 (December) or end of season. Example: baseline sprint 8.2s → target 7.6s by March. Do not set a target before you have a baseline.
3
Record individual baselines in Coach Notes
The Coach Notes column is not for methodology descriptions. It is for athlete-specific data: "W5 baseline: Leila 7.8s / Sara 8.1s / Nour 7.6s / group avg 7.8s"
4
Re-test at the defined frequency
If your KPI says "monthly", test every month and update the Coach Notes. The Q1 review will compare W5 baseline vs current value to calculate progression.
"Reference only" is not a benchmark. It means the test was planned but the target was not set. Replace it with a real numeric value before the season starts.

Weekly Monitoring
📝 What to fill every Friday ›

The Weekly Follow-Up is filled at the end of each training week — every Friday before you leave. It takes 10–15 minutes and is the most important monitoring document in the system.

1
Sessions delivered vs planned
Enter the actual number of sessions completed. If you planned 4 and delivered 3, enter 3. Never enter the planned number in the actual column.
2
Content actual vs planned
Describe what was actually done, not what was planned. If you had to change the session due to a field conflict or athlete absence, describe what you did instead and why.
3
Athlete feedback
Brief observation about the group's energy, motivation and reaction. This is not a summary of what you did — it is what you observed about the athletes. Example: "High energy on day 1, fatigue visible on day 3 after the match."
4
Coach notes
Your diagnostic observation — what worked, what didn't, what you will adjust next week. This is the most valuable field. A specific note ("exercises with 2 tasks are too complex for U10, need to simplify") is worth 10 generic notes.
5
Status
Select: ✓ Completed / ~ Partial / ✗ Missed / ⏸ Rest. A "Partial" week should always have a coach note explaining what was missed and why.
The planned content column exists for a reason: do not erase or overwrite it with actual content. Both columns must be filled — the difference between them is the key diagnostic signal.
🔍 What a good coach note looks like ›
Excellent coach note — Volleyball U10
Volleyball
"Any exercise with 2 tasks or more is too complicated for them — needs to be more simple exercises, one information only. Will redesign W34 session to single-skill focus."
Excellent coach note — Basketball U14
Basketball
"Girls struggled with defensive rotations in 5v5 — the concept of help defense is not yet internalized. Will add 3v3 half-court game with defensive constraint next week before returning to 5v5."
Weak coach note (avoid): "Good session." / "Normal week." / "Everything went well."
These contain no diagnostic information and cannot help you or the director understand what happened.

Monthly Reporting
📈 Structure of the Monthly Report ›

The Monthly Report has 5 sections. Fill all sections — a partial report is scored lower at evaluation.

#SectionKey fieldsDirector reads this for
1Training Load & VolumeSessions, hours, completion rate, attendancePlan adherence, session delivery
2Competition & KPI ResultsCompetition results, KPI progress this monthPerformance direction, competition outcomes
3Athlete StatusAthlete numbers, injuries, motivation, welfareSafeguarding, group health, flags
4Plan vs RealitySelf-assessment 1–5, what worked, what to adjustCoach's analytical capacity, honesty
5Support & ResourcesEquipment needs, support requestsWhat the program needs to function
Session completion rate is calculated automatically: sessions actual ÷ sessions planned × 100. The target is ≥ 85%. Below 60% triggers a director review.
The self-assessment scores (1–5) should be honest. A score of 3/5 with a clear explanation is worth more than a 5/5 with no comment. The director uses these scores to understand your professional development trajectory, not to penalize.
📌 When to submit and what happens next ›
1
Submit by end of the following month
October report → submitted by end of November. The report is due after the month ends so you can include full-month data.
2
Director reviews within 2 weeks
Mohammed Benhamida reads every monthly report. Flags in Section 3 (welfare, injury) are acted on immediately.
3
Feed the data into next month's plan
The "What to adjust" field in Section 4 should directly inform your plan for the following month. If you write "reduce session duration for U10" — update your Annual Plan accordingly.

📅 The Q1 review: what will be checked ›

The Q1 formal evaluation takes place at end of December 2026. The director will verify the following checklist:

✓
4 SMART objectives written and linked to KPIs
One per active age group. Each must have a target value, a deadline and at least one linked KPI code.
✓
Coach Notes filled with Week 5 baseline values
Individual athlete values, not group averages only. Example: "Sprint W5: Layan 8.2s / Amani 7.9s / avg 8.0s"
✓
Weekly Follow-Up: all weeks from W1 documented
Status marker filled for every week, even if "Rest" or "Cancelled". Empty weeks are flagged as missing data.
✓
Monthly reports for October and November submitted
Both reports must be in the system by end of December. October report minimum fields: sessions delivered, attendance rate, self-assessment.
✓
Athletes Roster: all athletes have complete records
Date of birth, age group, nationality and age group assignment are mandatory for competition registration.
Preparation advice: Open the Evaluation tab in this system one week before Q1. It calculates your score live and shows exactly which gaps remain. Address the red items first.
